Go further

This page only reads labels stored in the file. These free tools look at the pixels themselves:

  • SynthID Detector (Google) — checks for invisible watermarks that survive screenshots and re-saving. Detects images from Google, OpenAI, NVIDIA, and Kakao tools. Free with a Google account; daily limits apply.
  • ZeroGPT AI Image Detector — gives a likelihood score based on how the image looks. Treat the score as a hint, not a verdict.
  • Reverse image search — Google Images or TinEye. Finds where else the photo appears online, under what names.

These services upload your image to their servers. Don't send them photos you wouldn't want to share.

What this can and can't tell you

  • An AI label is strong evidence. Tools like ChatGPT, Gemini, and Adobe Firefly write these labels on purpose.
  • No label proves nothing. Screenshots, re-saving, and most social media and messaging apps strip this information. A scammer's image will usually have none.
  • Plain metadata can be faked or copied. Content Credentials are cryptographically signed, so they're much harder to forge — but the absence of them still says nothing.
